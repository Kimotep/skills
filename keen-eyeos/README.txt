KEEN EYEOS
A design companion for Apple platform projects.
Created by Kim Tumaini Jørgensen
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT THIS IS

Keen EyeOS is a structured design skill for anyone building apps in the Apple ecosystem —
iOS, macOS, iPadOS, watchOS, and beyond. It exists to bridge the gap between having an
idea and knowing exactly how to execute it in native Apple UI.

It guides you through intent, structure, component selection, and handover — and produces
outputs you can actually use: wireframes, design briefs, developer specs, and device mockups.

It adapts to you. Whether you're new to design or a seasoned engineer, it meets you at your
level and scales its language, depth, and recommendations accordingly.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHY I BUILT THIS

Getting Apple platform design right is harder than it looks. Not because the tools are
bad — they're exceptional — but because the gap between "working UI" and "native-feeling UI"
is subtle and unforgiving. The wrong component in the right place. The right spacing that's
two points off. A navigation pattern that technically works but fights the platform.

I built Keen EyeOS because I kept seeing the same problems across projects: developers who
knew SwiftUI well but reached for the wrong component, designers who had great instincts but
didn't know what was actually possible, and product people who couldn't bridge the two.

This skill is my attempt to encode what good Apple platform design judgment looks like —
not as a rulebook, but as a thinking partner that asks the right questions in the right order.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHERE THIS COMES FROM

The decisions, questions, and recommendations in this skill are grounded in 15 years of
product and design work inside the Apple ecosystem — across iOS, macOS, and iPadOS, from
early concept through to live product.

That includes building digital products from scratch (architecture, CMS, APIs, native apps),
leading product and engineering teams through full cycles, and shipping features to audiences
measured in millions of users. It also includes a lot of time spent learning what actually
makes an Apple platform app feel right — not just functional, but considered.

The skill reflects a specific belief: that good design on Apple platforms isn't about
decoration. It's about understanding the contract between your product and the platform,
and making deliberate choices about where to follow it and where to diverge.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT IT PRODUCES

All outputs are .md files, designed to live alongside your project:

  keen-design-plan.md        Vision, principles, and design direction
  keen-wireframes.md         Annotated ASCII screen layouts
  keen-technical-handover.md Developer-ready component and layout spec
  keen-stage.md              Device-framed visual mockup

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

HOW TO USE IT

Start a session and the skill will walk you through a short setup — your experience level,
your project context, and what you want to walk away with. From there it routes to the
right sub-skill and asks focused questions to extract intent before making any recommendations.

For quick one-off questions ("which component should I use for X?") there's a fast path
that skips setup entirely and answers directly.

Sub-skills:
  Keen Vision    — Design direction and project north star
  Keen Feature   — New screen or flow design
  Keen Polish    — Fixing what feels off
  Keen Signal    — Design decisions shaped by technical constraints
  Keen Stage     — Device-framed visual output

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A NOTE ON SCOPE

Keen EyeOS is opinionated about native Apple UI. It will always recommend the system component
first, explain the tradeoff clearly when you're considering going custom, and flag when
something requires a workaround rather than a clean solution.

It does not cover React Native, Flutter, or cross-platform frameworks.
It does not replace a designer or a design system — it helps you think more clearly about
the decisions that feed into both.
